# Blog Post 3: The College Wage Premium

Question: Did the wage premium associated with a bachelor's degree or higher increase between May 2003 and May 2024?

- [Read the published article](https://ruibao1129.github.io/6400-rb/blog/posts/post3/).

## Obtain the microdata

Register for free access at https://cps.ipums.org/cps/ and create an extract:

1. Select Basic Monthly CPS, May only, for every year 2003–2024 (22 samples). Do not select ASEC.
2. Include `YEAR`, `MONTH`, `AGE`, `SEX`, `EMPSTAT`, `CLASSWKR`, `EDUC`, `EARNWT`, `EARNWEEK2`, and `UHRSWORK1`. Automatically included identifiers can remain.
3. Select CSV and rectangular person records.
4. Download the compressed data and codebook. Save the CSV gzip file as `data/raw/cps_00001.csv.gz` relative to this README. Keep the codebook privately alongside the extract. Extract numbers can differ; rename your file or update `data_file` in `index.qmd`.

Raw microdata are not included in the public repository. Replication requires an independently obtained IPUMS extract. Record your download date and extract version; revised releases may produce slightly different results.

## Methods

Keep ages 25–54, EMPSTAT 10 or 12, CLASSWKR 20–28 (wage/salary), SEX 1 or 2, positive EARNWT, positive EARNWEEK2 below its NIU code 999999.99, and UHRSWORK1 1–99. Hours-vary (997) and NIU (999) are excluded; 99 means 99 or more hours and is treated as 99.

Education: high school = EDUC 73; some college/associate = 81,91,92; bachelor's or higher = 111,123,124,125. Lower education is outside this analysis.

Hourly earnings = min(EARNWEEK2,2884.61)/UHRSWORK1, consistently using main-job earnings and hours for hourly and salaried workers. This is a constructed hourly earnings measure, not exclusively the reported hourly wage. The common nominal cap harmonizes the upper weekly limit; it does not remove every measurement difference across years.

Real earnings = hourly earnings × CPI(May 2024)/CPI(May survey year). May CPI-U values (BLS CUUR0000SA0, all items, U.S. city average, not seasonally adjusted) are included in the source and exported to `results/cpi_may.csv`. Values were transcribed from the official BLS table: https://data.bls.gov/timeseries/CUUR0000SA0?years_option=all_years .

All medians use EARNWT. The weighted median is the first observed wage whose cumulative weight reaches half the group total. College premium = 100 × (college median/high-school median − 1). Percent premiums are unchanged by the common within-year inflation adjustment.

## Checks and limitations

The source verifies May-only coverage and all 22 years, checks CPI completeness, and exports unweighted cell counts. The final sample contains 171,391 person-month records in the supplied extract. This is not a count of unique individuals over time.

`results/topcode_sensitivity.csv` compares education-group medians before and after applying the cap; the 66 overall group-year medians are unchanged. `results/full_time_sensitivity.csv` restricts usual main-job hours to at least 35. The overall premium changes from about 71.3% to 75.5%; the full-time comparison changes from about 71.9% to 70.4%, so the direction of the small endpoint change is sample-dependent.

No confidence intervals or causal effects are estimated. May-only samples contain sampling noise. Imputed earnings are retained because allocation flags were not included. Graduate degrees are included in the college group. Weighting does not remove differences in occupation, experience, or selection into employment.
